## Overview

**Outside**
Orcod rises from the northern coastal plain like a fortress born of necessity. It is a sprawling town where the scent of salt air mingles with smoke from forge fires and the earthy musk of livestock pens. Wooden palisades encircle the settlement, with stone watchtowers at regular intervals. Orcish warriors patrol these heights, their eyes scanning both the forested hills to the southwest where elven territory begins, and the northern horizon where the sea meets the sky. From each tower, a banner hangs with the image of a boar's tusk dripping blood, the sigil of [[Warlord Grommash Bloodtusk]] 

**Inside**
The town thrums with controlled chaos. Herders drive bleating goats and snorting worgs through muddy streets while fisher-folk haul in their daily catch. The ring of hammers on anvils echoes from the craftsman's quarter, and the air is thick with competing smells: drying fish, roasting meat, animal dung, and the sharp tang of worked metal. It feels altogether unlike the nomadic camps of the deeper steppes.

## Places of Interest

### Bloodtusk Hall (The Warlord's Palace)

The most imposing structure in Orcod sits at the town's heart, built atop a raised earthwork that forces visitors to look up at it. Bloodtusk Hall is a hybrid of orcish practicality and captured Imperial craftsmanship. Its foundation stones were quarried by skilled hands (possibly dwarven prisoners from past campaigns), but the structure above is unmistakably orcish in character.

The main hall is a long, rectangular building with a high timber roof supported by massive pillars, each one carved with scenes of Grommash's victories. Smoke holes in the ceiling vent the perpetual fire pit that dominates the center of the great room, where the warlord holds court and feasts with his war-captains. Trophy weapons line the walls.

The palace compound includes barracks for Grommash's personal guard (50 battle-hardened warriors who've proven their loyalty), a war room where strategy is planned on maps spread across a massive table made from a single enormous tree trunk, and a vault where tribute and spoils are kept under heavy guard. Behind the main hall sits Grommash's private quarters. The warlord values function over comfort, though he does maintain a small collection of books and scrolls taken from defeated enemies, which he studies with more interest than most would expect from an orc.

A practice yard occupies the eastern section of the compound, where warriors train daily. Grommash himself can often be seen here in the early morning, working through weapon drills with single-minded intensity.

### The Market

The economic heart of Orcod sprawls across the western quarter of town, a warren of stalls, pens, and semi-permanent structures. The market operates under Grommash's direct oversight. He takes a cut of all major trades, but in return provides security and enforces a rough justice that keeps outright banditry to a minimum.

The livestock pens dominate the southern edge, where worgs snarl and snap in reinforced enclosures while horses whinny nervously nearby. Herdsmen from the steppes bring their finest beasts here, knowing the Horde's insatiable appetite for war mounts. Worg handlers are a breed apart, their arms covered in scars from training the vicious wolf-like creatures.

Fisher-folk sell their catch in the northern section, near the coast road. The smell is overpowering, but the fish and shellfish are fresh. Some of the catch is salted or smoked on-site for transport inland.

The metalwork quarter sits in the market's heart, where captured or contracted smiths work alongside orcish crafters. Weapons, armor, tools, and horseshoes flow from these forges. A few particularly skilled smiths are actually slaves. Dwarves or human craftsmen who work under guard in exchange for their lives. Their weapons are marked with a special stamp and fetch premium prices.

Most intriguing is the "Leaf Corner," a small, carefully controlled section where elven traders from the Larrel Sovereignty come to conduct business. The elves sell medicinal herbs, fine woodwork, and sometimes even minor magical trinkets. They keep their visits brief and their expressions neutral, but the trade is lucrative enough that both sides maintain this uneasy arrangement. Grommash stations his most level-headed warriors here to prevent incidents that might disrupt this profitable exchange.

### The Tide House (Tavern and Inn)

The largest tavern in Orcod occupies a sturdy two-story building near the market. The ground floor is a raucous common room where off-duty warriors, merchants, and laborers drink fermented mare's milk, harsh spirits, and surprisingly good ale brewed by a goblin named Skritch who runs the place.

### The Bone Shrine

On the eastern edge of town stands a circular stone platform open to the sky, ringed by carved pillars. This is where shamans conduct rituals to honor the warrior-ancestors who earned their place in the orcish afterlife. Skulls of great warriors are displayed on poles around the shrine.

The shrine sees heavy use before military campaigns, when shamans divine favorable times for battle and seek the ancestors' blessing. But it's also where the community gathers for funerals, weddings, and the naming ceremonies for warriors who've earned their adult names through great deeds.

The head shaman is an ancient orc named [[Gul'kar One-Eye]], whose visions are said to be disturbingly accurate. 
### The Prison Yard

Near the southern gate, a compound of sturdy cages and cells holds prisoners of various kinds: enemies awaiting ransom or execution, criminals serving sentences, and skilled captives being "convinced" to work for the Horde. Conditions are harsh but not gratuitously cruel.

Some prisoners are put to work on construction projects or in the mines in the nearby Vidga Range. Others, particularly captured Imperial officers or elven rangers, are held for information or diplomatic leverage.

### The Harbor Watch

Though not a true harbor (the coastline here is too rough for major shipping), Orcod maintains a small pier and boat launch where fishing vessels put in and occasional coastal traders arrive. A squat stone tower overlooks this area, manned by warriors who watch for threats from the sea.

The watch maintains a signal fire that can be lit to warn of approaching fleets or to summon help from other coastal settlements.

**Notable NPCs:**

- [[Warlord Grommash Bloodtusk]] - The warlord himself: pragmatic, ambitious, and more cunning than most expect
- [[Gul'kar One-Eye]] - Ancient shaman, keeper of traditions and diviner of omens
- [[Skritch]] - Goblin tavern keeper, information broker
- [[Murgat Ironfist]] - Captain of Grommash's personal guard, utterly loyal
- [[Velara Quickleaf]] - Elven trade representative who makes regular visits to the Leaf Corner, hiding her distaste behind professional courtesy
- [[Thorgrin Chainbreaker]] - A captured dwarf smith who's grudgingly accepted his fate and now produces exceptional weapons in exchange for better treatment

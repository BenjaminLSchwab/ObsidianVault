## Visual Description

Edwin Took cuts an unusual figure - a round-bellied halfling who has clearly invested considerable effort into appearing dwarven. His most striking feature is an elaborate false beard, meticulously crafted from braided brown hair streaked with dignified gray, reaching nearly to his chest.

He stands barely three feet tall naturally, but his custom-made shiny black boots add a deceptive four inches with their hidden lifts.

His clothing runs toward the practical but well-made. His cheeks are a bit ruddy from enjoying ale.

## Backstory

Edwin Took grew up in a prosperous halfling community in what was then peaceful Empire territory, where his family ran a modest but respectable inn. He dreamed of creating the best inn in the land.

In his twenties, Edwin scraped together enough coin to open his own place: The Generous Bushel, a tavern he envisioned as the finest establishment in halfling country. He spared no expense - imported spirits, carved furniture, even a chandelier. For a few golden years, it thrived. Edwin was proud, successful, and respected in his community.

Then the war came.

 A Urokgard raiding party burned half the town. The Generous Bushel survived the flames but not the aftermath - there was simply no one left with coin to spend. Edwin sold what remained for a fraction of its worth and joined the river of refugees flowing away from the fighting.

For two years, he drifted between odd jobs. Brewery hand. Caravan guard. Stable mucker. Anything that would keep him fed.

Then he heard the rumors: some eccentric noble with more money than sense was building a fortress in the mountains.  The noble, [[Democritus Maximus]], was apparently obsessed with dwarf culture and sparing no expense to recreate it.

Edwin saw his chance. But he also recognized the problem - the noble wanted dwarves to staff his fantasy, and a halfling refugee with a failed business wasn't going to impress anyone.

The false beard was his first investment, commissioned from a traveling wigmaker with the last of his savings. The dwarven accent came from weeks of practicing with actual dwarf laborers at construction sites, trading drinks for lessons in their manner of speech. He invented a backstory - fifteen years brewing in [[Wanedorf]], trained by the legendary (and entirely fictional) Kromgrim Steelbarrel.

When Edwin Took arrived at [[Fort Maximus]] to interview for the quartermaster position, he wasn't a failed halfling tavern keeper. He was a dwarven brewing master with impeccable credentials and an air of gruff competence. He was hired on the spot.
## Character Traits

**Dedicated Craftsman** - Edwin takes his brewing and quartermasterly duties with utmost seriousness. He knows every barrel in his cellar, every spice in his stores, every supplier within three days' ride.

**Easily Wounded Pride** - Years of compensating for his height and species have left Edwin sensitive to any perceived slight. He interprets innocent comments as attacks and responds with defensive bluster.

**Surprisingly Generous** - Beneath the prickly exterior, Edwin has a soft spot for anyone he considers "good folk." He'll quietly forgive debts, slip extra rations to those in need, or stand up for the underdog.

**Detail-Obsessed** - Everything must be just so. The beard must be braided evenly. The books must balance to the copper. The ale must be served at precisely the right temperature. This perfectionism makes him excellent at his job but exhausting to work with.

**Performance Anxiety** - Edwin lives in constant low-level fear that someone will call out his halfling heritage in front of a crowd, or that his accent will slip at a crucial moment. This manifests as overcompensation and occasional snippiness.

## Example Dialogue

_When greeting familiar customers:_ "Aye, back again are ye? Well, the good barrel's been waitin'. Not the swill I serve to them what don't appreciate fine craft - no, no, this is from the deep stores. Come, sit, and we'll discuss proper refreshment."

_When someone comments on his height:_ "Height? HEIGHT?! I'll have ye know that I've stood shoulder to shoulder with miners what've delved deeper than yer grandfather's grandfather!

_When doing inventory:_ "Forty-seven barrels of the amber, sixteen of the dark... no, no, wait, that doesn't account for what we drew yesterday. Start again. Forty-seven... no, blast it all. Where's me ledger?

_When someone earns his respect:_ "Right then. Ye've proven yerself decent folk. That means somethin' here. There's a bottle I've been savin' - none of yer business for what - but I'm thinkin' it's time to crack it open. We'll drink proper-like, you and I. To good company in a world what's too often short on it."

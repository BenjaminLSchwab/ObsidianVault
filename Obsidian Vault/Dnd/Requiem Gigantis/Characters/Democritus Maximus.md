## Visual Description

Democritus is a somewhat gaunt human in his early seventies with silver hair that he keeps "authentic dwarven braids". His once-distinguished features have softened with age and good living, leaving him with rosy cheeks perpetually flushed from wine and enthusiasm. He wears tailored vests embroidered with geometric patterns he claims are "traditional Steelbeard motifs," along with sturdy boots with brass buckles.

Most noticeably, he sports an elaborate beard that he keeps sectioned into multiple braids adorned with bronze rings and gem-studded clasps. His fingers are heavy with rings bearing what he insists are ancient dwarven symbols. He carries a walking stick carved from mountain oak with a head shaped like a tiny anvil, which he pounds on the ground for emphasis during his frequent monologues about dwarven superiority.

## Backstory

Born into the influential Maximus family during the waning years of [[The Holy Shard Empire]]'s glory, Democritus was raised in [[Passerelle]]'s merchant quarter, where his family controlled significant holdings in trade warehouses and property. As a young man of means with little inclination for actual work, he took the Grand Tour expected of Imperial nobility. Democritus became utterly enchanted during a three-week visit to Steelbeard Bastion.

What was meant to be a brief stopover became a formative obsession. He was captivated by dwarven engineering, their warrior culture, their craftsmanship, and—perhaps most importantly—the respect and stability their society seemed to embody compared to the fragmenting Empire. The dwarves themselves tolerated his enthusiasm with bemused patience, selling him various artifacts and trinkets at significantly marked-up "foreign collector" prices.

Returning to Passerelle, Democritus became insufferable, constantly comparing everything unfavorably to dwarven methods. His business acumen was mediocre at best—he was too busy redesigning his townhouse with "authentic dwarven stonework" to notice the Empire's decline. When the Urokgard began their aggressive expansion and Passerelle became a war zone, Democritus saw the writing on the wall. He liquidated his holdings at a loss (though still walked away wealthy), purchased a remote mountain property in the Titan Steps Range, and commissioned Fort Maximus.

The "fortress" was built by human contractors who humored his demands for "proper dwarven engineering" while cutting corners wherever he wouldn't notice. He filled it with his collection, hired a staff (including the opportunistic Gwimbly, whose fake-dwarf act Democritus hasn't detected), and settled into comfortable retirement. He occasionally hosts fellow Imperial nobles fleeing the collapse, regaling them with stories of Steelbeard Bastion until they make excuses to leave.

Despite his wealth and comfort, there's a melancholy beneath Democritus's bluster. He retreated to his mountain fastness because the world he knew is crumbling, and his dwarven obsession is partly a coping mechanism—clinging to a culture that seems eternal and unchanging when everything else is chaos.

## Character Traits

- Genuinely hospitable and generous to guests
- Enthusiastic and passionate about his interests
- Completely oblivious to how annoying his dwarven obsession is
- Tone-deaf about social cues; will monopolize conversations
- Somewhat cowardly—fled rather than face the war

## Example Dialogue

**When showing off his collection:** "Ah, this piece here—_magnificent_, isn't it? Acquired it from a dwarven trader who swore it was forged in the deep halls during the Second Age. Cost me a small fortune, but what's gold compared to preserving true artistry? You know, when I visited Steelbeard Bastion, I saw the great forges myself. The heat, the rhythm of the hammers, the _smell_ of honest work! Not like these soft Imperial craftsmen with their delicate filigree. Dwarves understand that beauty comes from _strength_, lad!"

**When discussing current events:** "The Urokgard? _Bah!_ Barbarians with no appreciation for proper stonework or craftsmanship. You know what a dwarf would do with that rabble? _[pounds walking stick]_ They'd hold a mountain pass with fifty warriors and send them running! I saw the defenses at Steelbeard Bastion—_impregnable_, I tell you! The Empire could learn a thing or two, if our weak-kneed priestess would listen. But no, we've gone soft, forgotten the old ways. That's why I've retreated here, to preserve _real_ culture!"

**When someone disagrees with him:** "No, no, no, lad, you misunderstand! I've _been_ to Steelbeard Bastion, spent three weeks among the dwarves themselves! They taught me their ways, shared their wisdom. The clan-father himself—well, perhaps not himself, but one of his bannermen—or was it a tavern keeper?—regardless, a dwarf of great standing told me personally that... what was I saying? Ah yes, dwarven methods are _always_ superior. It's simply _fact_!"

**When drunk (frequently):** "_[somewhat maudlin]_ You know what the problem is? The problem is... _[taps stick]_ ...the problem is we've lost our _foundation_. The Empire's crumbling like cheap mortar. My father's father helped build Passerelle into something _grand_, and now? Orcs in the streets! But the dwarves... the dwarves endure. Steelbeard Bastion stood for _thousands_ of years. That's what I'm doing here, you see? Building something that'll _last_. Something with _proper foundations_... _[trails off, staring into his cup]_"

**Interacting with Gwimbly:** "Gwimbly! Fetch us another cask from the cellar, there's a good fellow! You see, this lad's grandfather came from the Bastion region—isn't that right, Gwimbly?  That's why he understands _proper service_, unlike these shuffling human servants. The dwarves, you know, they treat their retainers like _clan_..."
